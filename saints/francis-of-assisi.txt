↳ bio
feast day: October 4
1181 | born in Assisi to a wealthy cloth merchant — grows up in comfort before His conversion
1205 | hears Christ speak from the San Damiano crucifix ("rebuild my church") — the turning point toward radical poverty
1209 | founds the Order of Friars Minor, formalizing a life of poverty and preaching
1212 | [Clare of Assisi](post.html?p=saints/clare-of-assisi.txt) joins His way of life, founding the Poor Clares — His message reaches beyond men
1219 | crosses into Egypt during the Crusades to meet Sultan al-Kamil — a rare act of interreligious peacemaking
1223 | creates the first live Nativity scene at Greccio — starts a devotional tradition still practiced today
1224 | receives the stigmata at La Verna — the first recorded case in Church history
1226 | composes the Canticle of the Creatures, nearly blind — the earliest known poem in the Italian vernacular
1226 | dies at the Porziuncola on October 3 (feast kept October 4)
1228 | canonized by Pope Gregory IX, only two years after death

↳ what resonates with me
* my own devotion to Francis and to others in His order, like [St. Anthony of Padua](post.html?p=saints/anthony-of-padua.txt), points to a kind of spirituality many people follow without even knowing it. it's not necessarily religious — it's simplicity, minimalism, care for the environment, sometimes it's even stoicism.
* if He were alive today, Francis would have the same concerns: harmony with creation, peace, refusal to accumulate — not as asceticism but as freedom for the essential.
* the context changes, not the diagnosis. to me, it's the most contemporary saint of them all. He could be alive today and preach the same exact concepts.
* Franciscan poverty and Japanese Zen philosophy converge on the same idea for example: less, to live more intensely.
* His Canticle of the Creatures touches deep into my soul. i do recognize an intense connection with nature, as my most calming place. it is not sentimentalism toward nature: it is precise moral recognition. every creature is "Laudato si'" because it reflects God, not because it is pleasant. it's also a case study in gratitude as a moral act of discernment, not sentimentalism.
* the Franciscan walk as spiritual practice and Thoreau's "saunter" converge too: same abandonment of the domestic, same openness to the essential in movement.
* i want to make it a habit to go there at least twice a year, in autumn and spring — just sit, in the Basilica or in San Damiano as well. i know God is everywhere, but somehow it's easier to connect in places like that.
* His broader legacy: the Order of Friars Minor and the Poor Clares still exist as living communities eight centuries later; the Nativity scene He improvised at Greccio in 1223 became a tradition still set up every December; even the Canticle of the Creatures, composed nearly blind near the end of His life, stands as the first poem in the Italian vernacular — !!a founder of religious orders and, almost by accident, of Italian literature!!. few figures leave that wide a footprint without ever writing a theological treatise.
