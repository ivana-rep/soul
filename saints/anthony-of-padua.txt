↳ bio
feast day: June 13
1195 | born in Lisbon, Portugal as Fernando Martins de Bulhões, to a noble and wealthy family
1210 | enters the Abbey of São Vicente de Fora, joining the Canons Regular of [St. Augustine](post.html?p=saints/augustine-of-hippo.txt) near Lisbon
1212 | transfers to the Monastery of the Holy Cross in Coimbra, seeking distance from distracting visits, and studies theology and Latin
1219 | ordained a priest in the Augustinian order
1220 | witnesses the relics of five Franciscan friars martyred in Morocco pass through Coimbra — moved to join the Franciscans Himself, taking the name Anthony
1220 | sails to Morocco as a Franciscan missionary hoping for martyrdom; falls seriously ill and is forced to turn back — a storm blows His ship instead to Sicily
1221 | attends the Franciscan general chapter in Assisi (the "Chapter of Mats"), where [Francis](post.html?p=saints/francis-of-assisi.txt) is present
1222 | improvises a sermon of remarkable depth at an ordination in Forlì when no other friar is prepared — His hidden gift for preaching is discovered
1224 | [Francis of Assisi](post.html?p=saints/francis-of-assisi.txt) entrusts Him with teaching theology to the friars, becoming the first Franciscan to do so
1226 | appointed provincial minister of northern Italy, leading friars while still preaching widely
1228 | meets Pope Gregory IX in Rome, who had been a close friend and adviser of Francis
1231 | settles in Padua, drawing enormous crowds with His preaching against usury and injustice
1231 | dies on June 13 at Arcella, near Padua, at 35
1232 | canonized by Pope Gregory IX at Spoleto — less than a year after His death, one of the fastest canonizations in Church history
1263 | His tomb is opened 32 years after death; His tongue is found still incorrupt, taken as a sign of His gift for preaching
1946 | declared a Doctor of the Church by Pope Pius XII, titled Doctor Evangelicus

↳ what resonates with me
* being in the same order as [Francis](post.html?p=saints/francis-of-assisi.txt) is part of what draws me to Anthony — He crossed from the Augustinians to the Franciscans almost on impulse, moved by the sight of five martyred friars passing through Coimbra, without knowing yet what He would become inside that order. Francis Himself later recognized His gift and entrusted Him with teaching theology, the first Franciscan ever asked to do so. it's a small lineage of recognition: one saint noticing the hidden gift of another.
* His gift for preaching wasn't planned — at an ordination in Forlì in 1222, with no other friar prepared, He was asked to improvise, and what came out revealed a depth no one expected, least of all Himself. !!the things you're actually made for often surface sideways!!, in a moment you didn't choose, not in the path you deliberately built. thirty-two years after His death His tomb was opened and His tongue was found still incorrupt — read, understandably, as a sign of that same gift.
* i live close to Padua and go to the Basilica often — it carries the same peace Assisi does for me, even without the hills and the quiet of nature around it. passing close to His tomb is always touching: so many believers there, praying hard, asking for something, for miracles too — you can feel, plainly, that this is a saint who really touched people, an intensity of devotion i don't see everywhere else. maybe it's less about the setting and more about what both places hold: the trace of someone who spent themselves fully on one thing, preaching in Anthony's case, poverty in Francis's, and left the place changed by it.
* His broader legacy: named a Doctor of the Church seven centuries after His death — one of the few given that title for preaching itself, not for founding an order or settling a doctrinal dispute, !!Doctor Evangelicus, doctor of the Gospel!!. the discovery of His incorrupt tongue thirty-two years after death became its own argument for the same claim: what set Him apart wasn't a body of writing left behind, but a voice — and even His body kept making that case once He'd stopped speaking.
