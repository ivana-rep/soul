↳ bio
feast day: August 14
1894 | born Rajmund Kolbe on January 8 in Zduńska Wola, in the part of Poland then ruled by Russia, son of a weaver and a midwife; baptized the same day
c. 1904 | as a boy, according to a letter His mother wrote after His death, He sees the Virgin Mary offering Him two crowns, a white one for purity and a red one for martyrdom, and answers that He accepts both
1907 | enters the Conventual Franciscan minor seminary in Lwów with His brother Franciszek
1910 | begins the novitiate and takes the name Maximilian
1912 | sent to Rome to study at the Gregorian university, where He earns a doctorate in philosophy
1914 | takes His solemn vows in Rome, adding the name Maria
1917 | on October 16, in Rome, founds the Militia Immaculatae with six fellow friars, after watching anti-papal demonstrations by freemasons in the city
1918 | ordained a priest in Rome, at Sant'Andrea della Valle
1919 | completes a doctorate in theology and returns to Poland
1920-1921 | falls seriously ill with tuberculosis, the disease that had killed two of His brothers, and spends long months in sanatoriums
1922 | in Kraków, publishes the first issue of the magazine Rycerz Niepokalanej ("knight of the Immaculata"), 5,000 copies
1927 | founds Niepokalanów near Warsaw: by 1939 it is one of the largest friaries in the world, with about 700 friars, its own printing works and, later, a radio station
1930 | sails to Japan and lands in Nagasaki, where a month later He prints a Japanese edition of the magazine
1931 | opens the friary of Mugenzai no Sono ("garden of the Immaculata") outside Nagasaki; built on the far side of a hill, it survives the atomic bomb in 1945
1936 | returns to Poland for good and takes charge of Niepokalanów again
1920s-1930s | alongside devotional material, the publications He leads carry anti-masonic articles and some antisemitic content, common in the Polish Catholic press of the time; historians still discuss how much of it was His own
1939 | arrested by the Germans in September with other friars and released in December; refuses to sign the list that would register Him as German; Niepokalanów takes in refugees, Jews among them
1941 | arrested by the Gestapo on February 17 and held in Pawiak prison, in Warsaw
1941 | on May 28 transferred to Auschwitz, prisoner 16670
1941 | at the end of July, after a prisoner escapes, the deputy commandant picks ten men to die of starvation; when one of them, Franciszek Gajowniczek, cries out for his wife and children, Kolbe steps forward and asks to die instead of him
1941 | on August 14, after two weeks in the starvation bunker of block 11, He is one of the last four still alive and is killed with an injection of carbolic acid; He is cremated the next day, the feast of the Assumption
1971 | beatified by Pope Paul VI as a confessor; Gajowniczek is present
1979 | at Auschwitz-Birkenau, Pope John Paul II calls Him "the patron of our difficult century"
1982 | canonized by Pope John Paul II on October 10 as a martyr, against the advice of the commission that had judged He was not killed for the faith; Gajowniczek is again in St Peter's square
1995 | Franciszek Gajowniczek dies at ninety-three, fifty-four years after the day Kolbe took his place

↳ quotes
\ first of all, avoid sadness and worries, because there is no reason to be sad. is not the world guided by divine providence, after all? can something happen that God does not know or does not allow? but if He allows it, then without doubt it is for our own good.
~ — letter to brother Henryk Borodziej in Nagasaki, early 1941, a few weeks before His arrest (Writings, no. 948)

\ hatred is not a creative force. only love is creative. these sufferings will not overcome us; they will only melt and temper us.
~ — said in Auschwitz to a fellow prisoner, Józef Stemler, a few weeks before His death, as Stemler later reported

\ I am a Polish Catholic priest. I want to die for him: he has a wife and children.
~ — at the roll call in Auschwitz, July 1941, as remembered by witnesses; the exact wording varies between accounts

\ without sacrifice there is no love.
~ — Writings, no. 503

\ we don't know what tomorrow holds. but we don't even need to know: it's enough that the Immaculata knows.
~ — Writings, no. 896

\ whoever has Mary for a mother has Christ for a brother.
~ — widely attributed to Him, and He is said to have loved repeating it, but not traceable to any specific writing

↳ what resonates with me
* a Franciscan of the twentieth century: He used the newest tools of His time, mass printing and radio, the way [Francis of Assisi](post.html?p=saints/francis-of-assisi.txt) used the roads and the squares of His.
* the two crowns come from a child's vision, told by His mother only after He died. on their own they sound like a pious legend; next to Auschwitz they read differently. a man who lived poor and chaste for forty years and then died for a stranger is exactly what the white crown and the red one describe. whatever happened that day, the life matched the promise.
* He had tuberculosis from His twenties and was often sick, and still He built one of the largest friaries in the world, then started again in Japan with almost no money and no Japanese. His health never decided what He could take on; the only question He asked was what He was being asked to do.
* He didn't know Gajowniczek. the man wasn't a friend, a friar, or someone He was responsible for. "there is no greater love than to lay down one's life for one's friends" ([John 15:13](post.html?p=bible/john_15-1-27.txt#v13)): Kolbe turned a stranger into a friend by dying for him. and Gajowniczek lived fifty-four more years, long enough to be in St Peter's for both the beatification and the canonization. !!one life given away, a whole life given back!!.
* the letter on sadness was written in early 1941 to a friar stranded in Japan by the war, a few weeks before His own arrest, when He had every reason to be afraid. it isn't optimism. it's the logic of [Romans 8:28](post.html?p=bible/romans_8-18-28.txt#v28): if God allows something, it is for our good. serenity, for Him, was a decision built on trust, not a mood.
* "hatred is not a creative force" was said in Auschwitz, where hatred looked like the only force at work. He didn't deny the evil around Him; He refused to answer it in kind, which is what Paul asks in [Romans 12:21](post.html?p=bible/romans_12-19-21.txt): conquer evil by doing good.
* His legacy: canonized as a martyr of charity in 1982 and called by John Paul II "the patron of our difficult century"; patron of prisoners, drug addicts, families, journalists and amateur radio operators; the Militia Immaculatae He started with six friars in Rome still exists worldwide, and both Niepokalanów and Mugenzai no Sono are still running.
