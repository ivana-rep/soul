the sequel to the gospel of Luke, by the same author and addressed to the same Theophilus: where the gospel tells what Jesus began to do and teach, Acts tells how His followers carried it on after His ascension. it covers roughly thirty years, from Jerusalem to Rome, and in some passages the narrator switches to "we", as if he had travelled with Paul himself.

its outline is given by Jesus in the first chapter: His followers will be His witnesses in Jerusalem, throughout Judea, in Samaria, and to the ends of the earth. chapters 1–7 are Jerusalem: the coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost, the first community sharing everything, the apostles arrested, Stephen the first martyr. chapters 8–12 are the widening circle: Philip in Samaria, Saul's conversion on the road to Damascus, Peter at the house of the Roman centurion Cornelius, the first gentiles welcomed. chapters 13–28 follow Paul: his missionary journeys, the council of Jerusalem deciding that gentiles need not become Jews to follow Christ, his arrest, and the voyage to Rome.

it ends open, with Paul under house arrest in Rome, still boldly proclaiming the Kingdom of God, with no one trying to stop him. it is the story of the church's beginnings, and of a Spirit that keeps pushing it beyond its own borders.
