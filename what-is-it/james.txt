a letter traditionally attributed to James, the brother of Jesus and leader of the church in Jerusalem — though some scholars question this attribution. it is addressed to "the twelve tribes scattered abroad", most likely Jewish Christians living outside the land of Israel, among them many poor believers facing trials and the arrogance of the rich.

it moves more like a collection of teachings than a linear argument. chapter 1: trials that produce endurance, asking God for wisdom, being quick to listen and slow to speak, doers of the word and not hearers only. chapter 2: no favoritism toward the rich, and faith without works is dead. chapter 3: the power of the tongue, and the wisdom that comes from above. chapter 4: quarrels, pride, and the call to submit to God and come close to Him. chapter 5: a warning to the rich who exploit, patience until the Lord comes, and the power of prayer.

it echoes Jesus' own teaching, especially the Sermon on the Mount, more than any other letter. Luther famously called it "an epistle of straw", but its point does not contradict Paul: real faith shows itself in what it does.
