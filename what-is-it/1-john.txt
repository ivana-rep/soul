a letter whose text never names its author; tradition attributes it to the apostle John, and it shares the language of the gospel of John — light and darkness, truth, love, life. it was probably written late in the first century, to churches shaken by a split: a group had left the community, denying that Jesus Christ had truly come in the flesh. the author writes so that those who remained may know that they have eternal life.

rather than a straight argument, it spirals around a few themes, returning to them each time more deeply. chapters 1–2: God is light, and walking in the light means being honest about our sin — if we confess it, He is faithful to forgive. chapters 3–4: we are God's children, and the proof is love for one another; God is love, and perfect love drives out fear. chapter 5: faith in the Son overcomes the world, and gives confidence when we pray.

it is where the new testament says it most simply: God is love. and it makes that love the test of everything else — not a feeling, but laying down one's life for others.
