the second book of the bible, traditionally attributed to Moses, and the story of his life's work. it picks up where Genesis left off: Jacob's family, welcomed into Egypt in Joseph's time, has grown into a people — first seen as a threat by a new Pharaoh, then turned into a nation of slaves. the God who made promises to Abraham seems silent, until He hears their groaning and remembers His covenant.

it moves in three parts. chapters 1–15 are the rescue: Moses's call at the burning bush, where God reveals His name — I AM who I AM —, the plagues, the passover, the crossing of the sea. chapters 16–18 are the wilderness: a freed people learning to trust God for bread and water one day at a time. chapters 19–40 are Sinai: the covenant, the ten commandments, the golden calf that nearly breaks it all, and the tabernacle — a tent built so that God could live among His people.

it is the defining story of the old testament: a God who saves before He commands. the passover becomes the setting of the last supper, and Jesus' death and resurrection a new exodus, out of a slavery deeper than Egypt.
