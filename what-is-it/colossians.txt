a letter bearing the name of the apostle Paul, written from prison to the church in Colossae, a small town in the Lycus valley, in what is now western Turkey. Paul had not founded this church and had never visited it: it was started by Epaphras, one of his co-workers. many scholars think it was written by a disciple of Paul, while others hold it is Paul's own. it answers a teaching spreading in the community — a "philosophy" mixing special rules on food and festivals, the worship of angels and harsh treatment of the body — that made faith in Christ alone seem not enough.

it unfolds in two parts. chapters 1–2 are about who Christ is: in an early hymn, the image of the invisible God, through whom and for whom all things were created, in whom all things hold together, the head of the church; and so, in Him, believers already have everything — no need for extra rules or powers. chapters 3–4 are what follows: set your hearts on things above, put off the old self, clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility and love, whatever you do, do it for the Lord; then household instructions and greetings, including Onesimus, the slave at the center of the letter to Philemon.

a short letter with a single message: Christ is enough, and life in Him is already complete.
