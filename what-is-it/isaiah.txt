one of the major prophets, Isaiah son of Amoz, who preached in Jerusalem in the 8th century bc, under four kings of Judah, while the Assyrian empire swallowed the northern kingdom of Israel and besieged Jerusalem itself. his call came in the year king Uzziah died, in a vision of the Lord on His throne. many scholars think the later chapters come from prophets in his tradition, speaking to the exiles in Babylon two centuries on; others read the whole book as his.

chapters 1–39 are judgment and hope in the Assyrian years: Judah's injustice and empty worship, the sign of Immanuel, the child called wonderful counselor, the shoot from the stump of Jesse, and the siege of Jerusalem under Sennacherib. chapters 40–55 open with "comfort my people": a message to the exiles that their time of punishment is over, that God is about to bring them home — naming Cyrus of Persia as His instrument — and the songs of the servant who suffers for others. chapters 56–66 look to the restored community and end with new heavens and a new earth.

among the most quoted books in the new testament. Jesus announced His mission in the synagogue of Nazareth by reading from it, and the church has always read the suffering servant of chapter 53 as His portrait.
