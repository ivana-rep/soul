a prophet of Judah, Joel son of Pethuel, about whom nothing else is known. the book names no king, so its date is uncertain: proposals range across several centuries, and many scholars place it after the exile. what is clear is the occasion — a devastating locust plague, followed by drought, that stripped the land bare and stopped the temple offerings. Joel reads it as a warning of a greater day of the Lord.

chapter 1 describes the plague and calls priests and people to lament. chapter 2 turns it into the image of an invading army and then into an invitation: rend your hearts, not your garments, and return to the Lord, who is gracious and merciful. God answers with a promise to restore the years the locusts ate, and then to pour out His Spirit on all people — sons and daughters, old and young, even servants. chapter 3 announces judgment on the nations in the valley of Jehoshaphat and a future in which God dwells in Zion. (in Hebrew and Italian bibles the outpouring of the Spirit is a chapter of its own, so the book has four chapters.)

on the day of Pentecost, Peter explained what was happening by quoting Joel's promise of the Spirit, and Paul quotes its call: everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.
