the third gospel, traditionally attributed to Luke — a physician and travelling companion of Paul, not an eyewitness himself, who says he carefully investigated everything from the beginning and set it down in order. it is addressed to a certain Theophilus, and written above all for gentile readers; many scholars date it around the years 80–90. it is the first half of a two-volume work: Acts is its sequel.

it is built as a journey. chapters 1–2 are the births of John the Baptist and Jesus, with Mary's song and the shepherds. chapters 3–9 are the preparation and the ministry in Galilee, opened in the synagogue of Nazareth with good news for the poor and freedom for captives. chapters 9–19 are the long road to Jerusalem, which holds most of the material found only in Luke: the good Samaritan, the lost coin, the prodigal son, Zacchaeus. chapters 19–24 are Jerusalem: the passion, the resurrection, and the two disciples walking to Emmaus.

it is the gospel of mercy for those on the margins — the poor, women, sinners, foreigners — and of prayer and the Holy Spirit. its story doesn't end with the ascension: it carries straight on into Acts, from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth.
