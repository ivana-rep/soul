the sixth book of the bible, named after Joshua — Moses's assistant and successor, chosen to lead Israel across the Jordan into the land promised to Abraham. Jewish tradition attributed it to Joshua himself; many scholars see it as part of a longer history, from Deuteronomy to Kings, that took its final shape much later, when Israel had lost the land and needed to understand why.

it moves in three parts. chapters 1–12 are the entry: the command to be strong and courageous, Rahab hiding the spies in Jericho, the crossing of the Jordan, the walls of Jericho falling, the defeat at Ai after Achan's theft, and the campaigns across Canaan — accounts of war that remain among the hardest pages of the bible to read. chapters 13–21 divide the land among the tribes, with cities of refuge for those who had killed by accident. chapters 22–24 are Joshua's farewell: gathering the people at Shechem, he puts the choice before them, declaring that he and his household will serve the Lord.

it is the story of a promise reaching the ground. in Hebrew, Joshua's name is the same as Jesus' — "the Lord saves" — and Rahab, the foreigner who sheltered the spies, appears in Jesus' genealogy in Matthew.
