the first half of what was originally a single book with 2 Samuel, divided in two only in the Greek translation. its author is unknown: Jewish tradition credited Samuel with its early chapters, and the prophets Nathan and Gad with the rest. it tells how Israel moved from a loose federation of tribes led by judges to a kingdom, around the 11th century bc, under pressure from the Philistines.

it is built around three figures. chapters 1–7 belong to Samuel: born in answer to his mother Hannah's prayer and called by God as a boy, at night, in the sanctuary at Shiloh, he becomes prophet and the last of the judges, while the ark is captured by the Philistines and then returned. chapters 8–15 belong to Saul: the people ask for a king like the other nations, Samuel warns them and anoints Saul, who soon disobeys and is rejected. chapters 16–31 belong to David: the shepherd boy chosen because the Lord looks at the heart, Goliath, the friendship with Saul's son Jonathan, years on the run from a jealous Saul whom David twice refuses to kill, and Saul's death on Mount Gilboa.

it is the beginning of David's line, from which the Messiah was expected to come. Hannah's song of a God who lifts up the lowly is echoed, more than a thousand years later, in Mary's Magnificat.
