Zechariah, son of Berechiah and grandson of Iddo, began to prophesy in 520 bc, the second year of the Persian king Darius, alongside the prophet Haggai. the exiles had come back from Babylon, but the rebuilding of the temple in Jerusalem had stalled for years. Zechariah's task was to rekindle their hope and get the work moving again, encouraging the governor Zerubbabel and the high priest Jeshua.

chapters 1–6 are a series of eight night visions — horsemen, horns, a measuring line, a golden lampstand between two olive trees, a flying scroll — each one explained by an angel: God has not forgotten Jerusalem and will finish what He started. chapters 7–8 answer a question about fasting with a call to justice and mercy, and a promise of joy. chapters 9–14, which many scholars think come from a later time, look further ahead: a humble king riding on a donkey, a rejected shepherd, one who is pierced and mourned, and the day the Lord becomes king over all the earth.

one of the minor prophets, and the longest of them. no other prophet is quoted as often in the gospels' account of Jesus' last days: the entry into Jerusalem on a donkey, the thirty pieces of silver, the struck shepherd, the pierced one.
