a letter from the apostle Paul to the church in Corinth, a busy and prosperous Roman port city in Greece, where he had founded the community and stayed about a year and a half. he writes a few years later, around the years 53–55, from Ephesus, answering two things: worrying news brought by members of Chloe's household, and a letter in which the Corinthians themselves had put questions to him.

it goes through the problems one by one. chapters 1–4 are about divisions — "i am a follower of Paul", "i follow Apollos" — and the wisdom of the cross, which looks like foolishness to the world. chapters 5–6 deal with sexual immorality and believers taking each other to court. chapter 7 answers questions about marriage, chapters 8–10 about food offered to idols and the freedom that must not trip up a weaker brother. chapters 11–14 are about worship: the Lord's supper, spiritual gifts, the church as one body with many parts, and in the middle, chapter 13, love as the more excellent way. chapter 15 is the resurrection, chapter 16 travel plans and greetings.

it is the most down-to-earth of Paul's letters: a real, divided, imperfect church, and the gospel applied to its everyday problems — with love above every gift, and the resurrection as the ground of everything.
