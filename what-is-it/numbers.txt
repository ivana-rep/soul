the fourth book of the bible, traditionally attributed to Moses. its name comes from the two censuses of the Israelites it records, one at the start and one near the end — two generations counted, because the first would not live to enter the promised land. it covers almost forty years in the wilderness, from Mount Sinai to the plains of Moab, across the Jordan from Jericho.

it moves in three stages. chapters 1–10 are the preparation at Sinai: the census, the order of the camp around the tabernacle, and the priestly blessing that asks the Lord to bless and keep His people. chapters 10–21 are the journey, with a crisis at its center: the people grumble, Miriam and Aaron challenge Moses, and when the spies return from Canaan the people refuse to go in, so that generation is sentenced to wander until it has died out; even Moses, striking the rock in anger, loses the right to enter. chapters 22–36 take place on the edge of the land: the prophet Balaam, hired to curse Israel, can only bless it; a new census is taken; Joshua is chosen to succeed Moses.

it is the story of a God who stays faithful to a people who keep failing Him. Jesus points to the bronze serpent lifted up in the desert as an image of His own cross, and Paul reads the wilderness years as a warning written for us.
