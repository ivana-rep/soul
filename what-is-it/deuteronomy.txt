Moses's final words to Israel, spoken on the plains of Moab just before the people cross the Jordan without him — the fifth and last book of the Torah. its name, from the Greek, means "second law": not a new law, but the covenant of Sinai retold to a new generation, the children of those who died in the wilderness, on the threshold of a land full of other gods. many scholars connect its final form with the book of the law found in the temple under king Josiah, in the 7th century bc, which sparked a great reform.

it is built as a series of speeches. chapters 1–4 look back on the journey from Sinai. chapters 5–26 are the heart: the ten commandments repeated, the call to love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul and strength, and the laws for life in the land. chapters 27–30 set out blessings and curses, ending with a choice between life and death, and the call to choose life. chapters 31–34 close Moses's life: Joshua commissioned, a final song and blessing, and Moses's death on Mount Nebo, looking out over a land he would not enter.

when He was tempted in the desert, Jesus answered three times with words from Deuteronomy; and it is the source of what He called the greatest commandment.
