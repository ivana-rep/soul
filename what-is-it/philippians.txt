a letter from the apostle Paul, written from prison, to the church in Philippi, a Roman colony in Macedonia and the first community he founded in Europe — the story, in Acts, of Lydia the seller of purple cloth and of the jailer converted at midnight. the place of his imprisonment is debated, Rome being the traditional answer. the Philippians had sent him a gift through one of their own, Epaphroditus, who fell gravely ill while with him; Paul writes to thank them, to reassure them, and to send Epaphroditus home.

it follows the shape of a letter between friends. chapter 1 is Paul's situation: chains that have actually helped the gospel spread, and his calm in the face of death — "living means living for Christ, and dying is even better". chapter 2 calls for humility and unity, around the early hymn of Christ who emptied Himself, took the form of a servant and was obedient to death on a cross. chapter 3 warns against trusting in the law, with Paul counting all his credentials as loss next to knowing Christ, and pressing on toward the goal. chapter 4 urges two women, Euodia and Syntyche, to agree, and turns to joy, prayer instead of worry, and contentment in every circumstance.

written in chains, it is Paul's most joyful letter: joy not dependent on circumstances, but rooted in Christ.
