a letter bearing the name of the apostle Paul, written from prison, traditionally to the church in Ephesus, the great city of the Roman province of Asia, where Paul had spent more than two years. some of the earliest manuscripts lack the words "in Ephesus", and many scholars think it was meant to circulate among several churches; many also think it was written by a disciple of Paul after his death, while others hold it is Paul's own, from his imprisonment in Rome in the early 60s.

it is built in two halves. chapters 1–3 are what God has done: chosen us in Christ before the creation of the world, made us alive when we were dead in our sins, saved us by grace through faith as a gift, and torn down the wall that divided Jews from gentiles, making them one new people. chapters 4–6 are how to live because of it: unity in one body, putting off the old self and putting on the new, speech that builds others up, relationships at home and at work, and finally the armor of God for a struggle that is not against flesh and blood.

less a reply to a crisis than a meditation on the church itself: one body, with Christ as its head, a sign of God's plan to bring all things together in Him.
