a letter to Titus, a gentile co-worker of Paul, who had been left on the island of Crete to put the young churches in order and appoint elders in every town. it is one of the three "pastoral letters", traditionally written by Paul, though many scholars think they come from a later disciple writing in his name. the churches on Crete were new, and troubled by teachers who were upsetting whole households.

chapter 1 lists the qualities of a good elder and warns against those who claim to know God but deny Him by their actions. chapter 2 gives instructions for older and younger men and women and for slaves, all grounded in one reason: the grace of God has appeared, and it teaches us how to live. chapter 3 turns to life in society — be good citizens, gentle with everyone — and recalls how God saved us not because of what we had done but because of His mercy, then warns against divisive people.

short and practical, it joins what the church believes to how the church lives: sound teaching is meant to show up as good conduct.
