the last book of the bible, written by a man named John, exiled on the island of Patmos "because of the word of God". tradition identifies him with the apostle John; many scholars think he was another early Christian prophet of the same name. it is usually dated around ad 95, under the emperor Domitian, though some propose an earlier date. it is addressed to seven churches in the province of Asia — Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia and Laodicea — small communities under pressure from Rome and tempted to compromise.

chapter 1 is a vision of the risen Christ. chapters 2–3 are letters to the seven churches, each praised, warned or encouraged. chapters 4–5 open onto the throne of God and the Lamb who was slain. chapters 6–16 unfold in series of seven — seals, trumpets, bowls — images of judgment on a world in revolt. chapters 17–20 tell the fall of "Babylon", the final defeat of evil and the last judgment. chapters 21–22 end with a new heaven and a new earth, where God lives with His people and wipes away every tear.

written in the symbolic language of apocalyptic, it is less a timetable of the future than a revelation of who truly rules history. it closes the bible where Genesis opened it — with the tree of life, and God dwelling with humanity.
