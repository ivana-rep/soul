Micah of Moresheth, a village in the Judean countryside, preached in the 8th century bc under kings Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah — a contemporary of Isaiah. while the Assyrian threat grew, he spoke against the powerful who seized fields and houses, judges who took bribes, prophets who preached for pay, and a worship that tried to cover all this with offerings. he foretold that Jerusalem would be plowed like a field; a century later, that prophecy was remembered and saved Jeremiah's life.

the book moves in three cycles, each opening with a call to "hear" (chapters 1–2, 3–5, 6–7), and each passes from judgment to hope. among the hope: nations beating their swords into plowshares, and a ruler coming from little Bethlehem. chapter 6 stages a trial between God and His people and condenses what He asks: to do what is right, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God. chapter 7 ends with a God who delights in mercy and throws our sins into the depths of the sea.

his name means "who is like the Lord?", and the book returns to that very question in its last lines. when the magi asked where the Messiah would be born, the answer came from Micah: Bethlehem.
