a short letter to the church in Thessalonica, sent in the name of Paul, Silas and Timothy, apparently soon after 1 Thessalonians. traditionally written by Paul, though many scholars think it may come from a later follower writing in his name. the community was still under persecution, and a new confusion had spread: some were saying that the day of the Lord had already come, and some had stopped working altogether.

it has three short chapters. chapter 1 encourages believers under pressure, promising that God will set things right. chapter 2 corrects the confusion: the day of the Lord has not come yet, and certain things must happen first, including the appearance of "the man of lawlessness". chapter 3 asks for prayer and confronts those living idly — whoever is unwilling to work should not eat.

its contribution is balance: hope in Christ's return is not a reason to stop living ordinary life, but the reason to keep working, keep doing good, and stand firm.
