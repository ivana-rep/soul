a letter to Timothy — a young man from Lystra, son of a Jewish mother and a Greek father, who became Paul's closest co-worker. Paul had left him in Ephesus to stop certain people from spreading false teaching. it is one of the three "pastoral letters", with 2 Timothy and Titus: traditionally written by Paul in the 60s ad, though many scholars think they come from a later disciple writing in his name.

chapter 1 sets out the problem — teaching that breeds speculation rather than faith — and Paul's own story as its answer: Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, and he counts himself the worst of them. chapters 2–3 order the life of the community: prayer for everyone, worship, and the character required of overseers and deacons. chapter 4 warns against a false, rule-bound spirituality and urges Timothy to train himself in godliness and not let anyone look down on him for being young. chapters 5–6 deal with widows, elders, slaves and money — the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil.

it is the new testament's handbook for a church that has to last: guarding the truth, choosing leaders, and caring for the vulnerable.
