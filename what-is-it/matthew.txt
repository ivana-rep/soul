the first gospel, traditionally attributed to Matthew, a former tax collector who left everything to become one of Jesus' twelve apostles. the text itself is anonymous; many scholars date it to the decades after the destruction of Jerusalem in ad 70 and think it draws on Mark's gospel. it speaks to a largely Jewish-Christian community, explaining how Jesus is the Messiah promised in Israel's scriptures.

it opens with a genealogy that traces Jesus back to David and Abraham (chapter 1) and the infancy stories of the magi and the flight to Egypt (chapter 2). the heart of the book is built around five great discourses, each closing with "when Jesus had finished saying these things": the sermon on the mount (chapters 5–7), the sending of the twelve (10), the parables of the kingdom (13), life in the community (18), and the end of the age (24–25). between them come His miracles and growing conflict, and chapters 26–28 recount the passion, the resurrection and the great commission.

again and again it notes that something happened "to fulfill" what the prophets said. it opens the new testament and frames Jesus between two promises: Emmanuel, God with us, at His birth, and "I am with you always" in its last verse.
