[Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_the_Apostle), also called Levi, was a tax collector in Capernaum, on the sea of Galilee: a trade that made Him a collaborator of Rome in the eyes of His own people. Jesus saw Him sitting at His booth and said "follow Me", and Matthew got up and followed ([Matthew 9:9](post.html?p=bible/matthew_9-9-13.txt#v9)); soon after, He invited Jesus to dinner at His home together with other tax collectors and sinners. He became one of the twelve apostles. according to church fathers such as Irenaeus and Clement of Alexandria, He preached first in Judea and then in other lands; tradition says He died a martyr, though the accounts disagree on where. His relics are venerated in the crypt of [Salerno cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salerno_Cathedral), His feast is September 21, and His symbol among the [four evangelists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Evangelists) is a winged man.

---

the first gospel, traditionally attributed to Matthew. the text itself is anonymous; many scholars date it to the decades after the destruction of Jerusalem in ad 70 and think it draws on Mark's gospel. it speaks to a largely Jewish-Christian community, explaining how Jesus is the Messiah promised in Israel's scriptures.

it opens with a genealogy that traces Jesus back to David and Abraham (chapter 1) and the infancy stories of the magi and the flight to Egypt (chapter 2). the heart of the book is built around five great discourses, each closing with "when Jesus had finished saying these things": the sermon on the mount (chapters 5–7), the sending of the twelve (10), the parables of the kingdom (13), life in the community (18), and the end of the age (24–25). between them come His miracles and growing conflict, and chapters 26–28 recount the passion, the resurrection and the great commission.

again and again it notes that something happened "to fulfill" what the prophets said. it opens the new testament and frames Jesus between two promises: Emmanuel, God with us, at His birth, and "I am with you always" in its last verse.
