[John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Apostle) was a fisherman on the sea of Galilee, son of Zebedee and younger brother of James: the two of them left their father in the boat to follow Jesus, who nicknamed them "[sons of thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boanerges)". together with Peter and James He was one of the three closest to Jesus, present at the transfiguration and in Gethsemane. tradition identifies Him with "the disciple whom Jesus loved", the one who leaned on Jesus at the last supper and to whom Jesus, from the cross, entrusted Mary. the church fathers say He later lived in Ephesus, was exiled to the island of [Patmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patmos), and was the only one of the twelve to die of old age rather than as a martyr; many modern scholars question parts of this picture. His feast is December 27, and His symbol among the [four evangelists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Evangelists) is the eagle.

---

the fourth gospel, traditionally attributed to the apostle John, who never names Himself in the book. many scholars date it to the end of the first century, and an early tradition places its writing in Ephesus. it says plainly why it was written: so that its readers may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and by believing have life in His name.

it opens with a prologue unlike any other gospel — "in the beginning the Word already existed" — in chapter 1. chapters 1–12 are often called the book of signs: seven miracles, from water turned into wine at Cana to the raising of Lazarus, each one pointing beyond itself to who Jesus is, alongside His "I am" sayings — the bread of life, the light of the world, the good shepherd, the resurrection and the life. chapters 13–20 are the book of glory: the last supper, with the washing of the feet and Jesus' long farewell to His disciples, then the cross and the resurrection. chapter 21 is an epilogue by the sea of Galilee.

fewer parables, fewer miracles, longer conversations: it is the most reflective of the four, written so that the reader doesn't just know what Jesus did, but who He is.
