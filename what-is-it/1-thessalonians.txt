a letter from the apostle Paul, with Silas and Timothy, to the church in Thessalonica, a busy port city in Macedonia. Paul had founded the community on his second missionary journey but was forced to leave after only a short stay, driven out by opposition. worried, he sent Timothy back; Timothy returned with good news — the believers were holding firm — and this letter is Paul's response, written from Corinth around ad 50–51. many scholars consider it the oldest surviving writing of the new testament.

it moves in two parts. chapters 1–3 are thanksgiving and memory: how the Thessalonians received the gospel, how Paul lived among them, his longing to see them again, his relief at Timothy's report. chapters 4–5 turn to instruction: a life of holiness and love, quiet work, and an answer to the question troubling them most — what happens to believers who die before Christ returns. it closes with short, dense exhortations: rejoice always, never stop praying, give thanks in every circumstance.

it shows the church at its very beginning, already living by faith, love and hope — the three words Paul names in its first chapter and again in its last.
