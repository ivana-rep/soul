a very short letter, just twenty-five verses, from "Jude, brother of James" — traditionally identified as one of the brothers of Jesus. its date and recipients are uncertain. the author had wanted to write about the salvation he shared with his readers, but felt he had to urge them instead to defend the faith, because certain people had slipped into the community and were turning God's grace into an excuse for immorality.

verses 1–4 explain the urgency. verses 5–16 describe the intruders through examples of judgment from Israel's history and tradition — the people in the wilderness, the fallen angels, Sodom and Gomorrah, Cain, Balaam, Korah — and include a quotation from the book of Enoch, a Jewish writing outside the bible. verses 17–23 turn to the believers: remember what the apostles foretold, build yourselves up in faith, pray in the Holy Spirit, show mercy to those who doubt. verses 24–25 close with one of the best-known doxologies of the new testament: to Him who is able to keep you from falling.

much of its middle section closely parallels the second chapter of 2 Peter.
