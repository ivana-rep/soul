set during Israel's exile in Babylon in the 6th century bc, it follows Daniel — a young Israelite deported by king Nebuchadnezzar to serve in the Babylonian court, known for interpreting dreams and for surviving the lions' den after refusing to stop praying. many scholars date its final form to around 165 bc, when the Seleucid king Antiochus IV was persecuting the Jews and desecrating the temple; tradition reads it as Daniel's own record. either way, it was written for believers living under hostile powers.

chapters 1–6 are court stories: faithfulness to God under pressure, the three friends in the fiery furnace, the writing on the wall at Belshazzar's feast, the lions' den. chapters 7–12 are apocalyptic visions of empires rising and falling as beasts, of one like a son of man receiving an everlasting kingdom, of the archangel Michael, and, at the end, of the dead who will rise. part of the book is written in Aramaic rather than Hebrew. catholic and orthodox bibles also include further passages preserved in Greek: the prayer in the furnace, Susanna, Bel and the dragon.

Jesus' favorite title for Himself, the Son of Man, comes from chapter 7, and the book of Revelation draws heavily on its visions.
