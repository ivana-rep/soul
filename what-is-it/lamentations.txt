five poems of grief over the fall of Jerusalem in 586 bc, when the Babylonian army destroyed the city and its temple and carried its people into exile. the book itself names no author; tradition attributes it to Jeremiah, the prophet who had warned that this would happen and lived through it. it is still read in synagogues on the day of mourning for the temple's destruction.

each chapter is one poem. chapters 1, 2 and 4 are acrostics — each verse begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet, 22 in all — as if to give order to a sorrow that has none. chapter 3, at the center, triples the pattern, three verses for each letter, and moves from a single man's despair to the turning point: the faithful love of the Lord never ends, His mercies begin afresh each morning. chapter 5 keeps the 22 lines but drops the alphabet, ending as a communal prayer that asks God to restore His people, still without an answer.

it gives scripture a language for grief that doesn't rush to resolution. centuries later Jesus, too, would weep over Jerusalem and foretell the destruction of its temple.
