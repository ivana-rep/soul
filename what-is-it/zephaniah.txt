Zephaniah, a prophet who traces his family back four generations to a Hezekiah — probably the king — preached in Jerusalem in the days of king Josiah, in the 7th century bc. after decades of idolatry under the previous kings, Judah worshiped other gods alongside the Lord and lived as if He would do nothing, good or bad. Zephaniah probably spoke before Josiah's great religious reform, and his words fit the climate that led to it.

chapter 1 announces the day of the Lord as a day of wrath against Judah and against all who are complacent; its image of that day inspired the medieval hymn dies irae. chapter 2 calls the humble to seek the Lord, perhaps to be sheltered, and turns to the surrounding nations, from Philistia to Assyria and its capital Nineveh. chapter 3 rebukes Jerusalem's leaders, then changes tone: God will purify the peoples, leave a humble remnant that trusts in Him, and the book ends with Him living among His people and rejoicing over them with songs.

one of the minor prophets, short but sharp. its call "sing, daughter of Zion… the Lord is in your midst" echoes, many readers have noted, in the angel's greeting to Mary.
