a story set in Susa, capital of the Persian empire, under king Xerxes (Ahasuerus) in the 5th century bc, among the Jews who had stayed in exile rather than return to Jerusalem. its author is unknown. it tells how a young Jewish orphan, Esther, raised by her cousin Mordecai, becomes queen of Persia and risks her life to save her people from a decree of extermination. in its Hebrew text God's name never appears — yet His providence runs through every turn.

it moves in three stages. chapters 1–2 set the scene: queen Vashti deposed, Esther chosen as queen while keeping hidden that she is Jewish, Mordecai uncovering a plot against the king. chapters 3–7 are the threat and the reversal: Haman, the king's highest official, offended because Mordecai will not bow to him, obtains a decree to destroy all the Jews; Mordecai urges Esther that she may have become queen for just such a time as this, and she goes to the king uninvited, ready to die — until Haman is hanged on the very gallows he had built for Mordecai. chapters 8–10 are the rescue: a counter-decree allows the Jews to defend themselves, and the feast of Purim is established to remember it.

it is never quoted in the new testament, but it carries one of the bible's most quietly powerful ideas: God at work even where He seems absent, through ordinary people willing to risk themselves for others.
