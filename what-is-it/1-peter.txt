a letter attributed to the apostle Peter — a fisherman who became one of Jesus' twelve closest followers — sent from "Babylon", most likely a code name for Rome, with the help of Silvanus. many scholars think it was written by a later disciple in his name. it is addressed to Christians scattered across Asia Minor, in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia and Bithynia, whom it calls foreigners and exiles: mostly converts from paganism, facing suspicion, slander and hostility for their faith.

it moves in three parts. from 1:1 to 2:10, identity: born again to a living hope, called to be holy, living stones built into a spiritual house, God's own people. from 2:11 to 4:11, life among outsiders: doing good, respecting authority, enduring unjust suffering after the example of Christ, who carried our sins in His body on the cross. from 4:12 to the end, the fiery trial: suffering as a sharing in Christ's suffering, elders who shepherd the flock willingly, humility, and casting every worry on God, because He cares for us.

it is the new testament's letter for suffering: not explaining it away, but placing it inside hope.
