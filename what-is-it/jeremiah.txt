Jeremiah, a priest's son from the village of Anathoth, called as a young man around 627 bc and active for some forty years, through the last kings of Judah and the fall of Jerusalem to Babylon in 586 bc. often called the weeping prophet, he spent his life warning a nation that would not listen: he was beaten, imprisoned, thrown into a cistern, and finally taken to Egypt against his will. his scribe Baruch wrote down his words, and when king Jehoiakim burned the scroll, they were dictated again.

the book is not in chronological order. chapter 1 is his call. chapters 2–25 are oracles against Judah's idolatry and false security, along with his raw prayers of complaint to God. chapters 26–45 tell the story of his life and include the book of consolation (chapters 30–33), with the promise of a new covenant written on the heart; chapter 29 is his letter to the exiles, telling them to build, plant and pray for Babylon. chapters 46–51 are oracles against the nations, and chapter 52 recounts the fall of Jerusalem.

the longest book of the bible by word count. Jesus speaks of the new covenant in His blood at the last supper, and the letter to the Hebrews quotes Jeremiah's promise in full.
