a prophet of Judah about whom almost nothing is known, writing in the late 7th century bc as the Babylonians were rising to power. Judah was full of violence and injustice, and God seemed not to act. Habakkuk doesn't preach to the people: he argues with God, and the book records the conversation.

chapters 1–2 are two complaints and two answers. first: how long will You let evil go unpunished? God replies that He is raising up the Babylonians to judge Judah. second: how can a holy God use a nation even more cruel? the answer is to write the vision down and wait for it, because it will come at the appointed time — and meanwhile the righteous will live by faithfulness. five woes follow against the oppressor, who will fall in his turn. chapter 3 is a prayer set to music: a vision of God coming in power, and a trust that holds even with no harvest at all — yet i will rejoice in the Lord.

the line "the righteous will live by faith" is quoted three times in the new testament, by Paul in Romans and Galatians and in the letter to the Hebrews, and it was the verse that set Martin Luther on the path of the reformation.
