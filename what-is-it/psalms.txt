a collection of 150 songs and prayers, written over many centuries and gathered into its final form after the exile, to be sung in the temple and prayed by the people. about half carry David's name — the shepherd who became Israel's king, and its most storied psalm-writer — while others are linked to Asaph, the sons of Korah, Solomon and even Moses. it is the prayer book of Israel, and still the prayer book of the church.

it is arranged in five books, often seen as an echo of the five books of Moses, each closing with a word of praise: Psalms 1–41, 42–72, 73–89, 90–106 and 107–150. inside them every kind of prayer stands side by side — praise, thanksgiving, trust, confession, songs for the king, wisdom, and above all lament, the most common type of all: complaint, anger, fear and grief brought straight to God, almost always turning, by the end, toward trust. the collection opens with the blessed life of one who delights in God's law, and ends in a crescendo of pure praise.

it is one of the books most quoted in the new testament. Jesus prayed it to the very end: on the cross He cried out the opening of Psalm 22, "my God, my God, why have You abandoned me?", and died entrusting His spirit into the Father's hands with words from Psalm 31.
