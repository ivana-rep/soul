the first half of what was originally a single book with 2 Kings; Jewish tradition attributed it to the prophet Jeremiah. it was completed during or after the exile in Babylon, and reads Israel's history with one question in mind: how did the people of God lose their land and their temple? the answer it gives is faithfulness — or the lack of it — to the one God.

it moves in three parts. chapters 1–11 are Solomon: his succession to David, his prayer for wisdom rather than riches, the building and dedication of the temple in Jerusalem, the visit of the queen of Sheba — and then his many foreign wives turning his heart toward other gods. chapters 12–16 are the split: under Solomon's son Rehoboam the ten northern tribes break away under Jeroboam, who sets up golden calves at Bethel and Dan, and a series of kings follows in both Israel and Judah, most of them judged to have done evil in the sight of the Lord. chapters 17–22 are dominated by the prophet Elijah: the drought, the widow of Zarephath, the contest with the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel, the flight to Horeb, where God speaks not in the wind or the fire but in a gentle whisper, and the conflict with king Ahab and Jezebel.

Jesus speaks of Himself as greater than Solomon, Elijah appears beside Him at the transfiguration, and John the Baptist is said to come in the spirit and power of Elijah.
