a letter to Timothy, Paul's closest co-worker, presented as written from prison in Rome near the end of Paul's life — traditionally his last letter, though, like the other pastoral letters, many scholars think it may come from a later disciple writing in his name. Paul is in chains, many have abandoned him, only Luke is still with him, and he asks Timothy to come quickly, bringing his cloak and his scrolls.

chapter 1 urges Timothy to fan into flame the gift God gave him — God has not given us a spirit of fear — and not to be ashamed of the gospel. chapter 2 uses images of the soldier, the athlete and the farmer to describe a faithful, enduring life. chapter 3 warns of hard times ahead and points to Scripture, inspired by God and useful for teaching, as the anchor. chapter 4 is a final charge — preach the word — and a farewell: i have fought the good fight, i have finished the race, i have kept the faith.

it reads as a last testament: one generation handing the faith on to the next, with nothing left to prove and everything left to entrust.
