a letter from the apostle Paul to the church in Corinth, written after a painful conflict between them. after 1 Corinthians, things had gotten worse: Paul made what he calls a painful visit, then sent a letter written "with many tears". now, from Macedonia, around the years 55–56, he writes with relief at news that most of the community has come back to him — though rival preachers are still undermining him. many scholars think the letter as we have it may combine more than one of Paul's letters.

it has three parts. chapters 1–7 are reconciliation, and a defense of what ministry really is: not strength on display but treasure in jars of clay, an outer self wasting away while the inner self is renewed day by day, the hope of an eternal home, and the message that anyone in Christ is a new creation. chapters 8–9 are an appeal for the collection Paul was gathering for the poor believers in Jerusalem, with God loving a cheerful giver. chapters 10–13 change tone sharply: a fierce, ironic defense against the so-called "super-apostles", in which Paul boasts only of his weakness — his sufferings, the thorn in his flesh, and the Lord's answer: "My grace is all you need".

it is Paul's most personal letter, and its great lesson is that God's power shows itself most fully in weakness.
