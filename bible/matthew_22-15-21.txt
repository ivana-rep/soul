---

the question is a trap set by two groups who couldn't stand each other: the pharisees, who resented roman rule, and the herodians, who did well out of it. if Jesus says yes, He sides with the occupier. if He says no, He's a rebel. either way, someone gets to claim God for their side.
Jesus asks for a coin and asks whose face is on it. caesar's. then give it back to caesar.
the second half of the answer is the one that cuts. the coin carries caesar's image; we carry God's (Genesis 1:27). the coin belongs to the one stamped on it, and so do we.
wanting God on our team is an old temptation: our party, our side of the argument, our interests. He doesn't take that role. He also doesn't stay out of the rest of our life: work, money and politics are His as much as the hours we spend praying. all of it goes back to Him.
God won't meddle in our fights. He did something else: He got involved with us.
