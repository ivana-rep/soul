[1] o Lord, how long will you forget me?
forever? how long will you look the other way?
[2] how long must i struggle with anguish in my soul,
with sorrow in my heart every day?
how long will my enemy have the upper hand?
[3] turn and answer me, o Lord my God!
restore the sparkle to my eyes, or i will die.
[4] don't let my enemies gloat, saying, "we have defeated him!"
don't let them rejoice at my downfall.
[5] but !!i trust in your unfailing love!!.
i will rejoice because you have rescued me.
[6] i will sing to the Lord because He is good to me.

---

murmuring and lament can sound the same. same discomfort, sometimes even the same words.
what makes them different isn't the complaint — it's where it's aimed, and what it's asking for.
murmuring goes sideways. at people nearby, or at God but behind His back. it's not asking for anything to change — it's asking to be agreed with. underneath it: distrust that God will provide, plus a quiet certainty i'm owed better. and it stays there. nothing moves.
lament goes straight at God. raw, even accusatory — but addressed. and it keeps moving: pain named, something asked for, trust reasserted. the complaint is a step inside the relationship, not a replacement for it.
Psalm 13 is the whole arc in six verses. verses 1-2 are the complaint, unfiltered — how long, repeated, no politeness. verses 3-4 turn it into a direct ask, still aimed at God. verses 5-6 land in trust — not because the circumstance changed, but because the complaint went somewhere instead of just sitting there.
the test i can actually use: is this going to someone who can act, and toward something? or is it just landing on whoever's nearby, with nowhere to go?
