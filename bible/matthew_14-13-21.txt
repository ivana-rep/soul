[13] as soon as Jesus heard the news, he left in a boat to a remote area to be alone.
but the crowds heard where he was headed and followed on foot from many towns.
[14] Jesus saw the huge crowd as he stepped from the boat, and !!he had compassion on them!! and healed their sick.
[15] that evening the disciples came to him and said, "this is a remote place, and it's already getting late. send the crowds away so they can go to the villages and buy food for themselves."
[16] but Jesus said, "that isn't necessary—you feed them."
[17] "but we have only five loaves of bread and two fish!" they answered.
[18] "bring them here," he said.
[19] then he told the people to sit down on the grass. Jesus took the five loaves and two fish, looked up toward heaven, and blessed them. then, breaking the loaves into pieces, he gave the bread to the disciples, who distributed it to the people.
[20] they all ate as much as they wanted, and afterward, the disciples picked up twelve baskets of leftovers.
[21] about 5,000 men were fed that day, in addition to all the women and children!

---

grief doesn't have to close you off — it can open you to service. Jesus had just heard that John the Baptist — his cousin, the one who baptized him and prepared the way for him — had been beheaded by Herod. he wanted silence to grieve, but the crowd found him anyway.
the disciples reason with scarcity: count the resources, and when the math doesn't work, send people away. "we only have five loaves and two fish" — as if the little was an answer instead of a starting point.
Jesus doesn't explain, he just says: bring them here. abundance isn't something you attract from outside — it's already inside what little you have, waiting to be released.
everyone ate until they were satisfied, and twelve baskets were left over. not scraps — surplus. abundance opens; hoarding closes. the difference was never the resources. it was how they looked at them.
