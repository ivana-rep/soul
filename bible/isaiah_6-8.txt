[8] then i heard the Lord asking,
"whom should I send as a messenger to this people?
who will go for us?"
i said, "!!here i am. send me.!!"

---

this moment comes right after Isaiah's vision of the Lord seated on His throne, seraphim calling out "holy, holy, holy," the temple shaking under their voices. Isaiah's first response isn't willingness — it's terror. "woe is me," he says, "i am a man of unclean lips," undone by seeing God's holiness next to his own. a seraph touches his mouth with a burning coal from the altar, taking away his guilt. only after that — after he's confronted, exposed, and cleansed — does he hear the Lord ask, "whom should I send? who will go for us?" and answer yes, before even knowing what the assignment is (the next verse hands him a hard message, to a people who won't want to hear it). the order matters: he isn't ready first and then meets God. the encounter is what makes him ready.
