\ an argument that goes back to Aristotle, restated by the philosopher Edward Feser, starts from a cup of coffee.
\ the coffee is hot right now, but it could become cold. philosophy calls the first actual — what something is — and the second potential — what it could become. one rule holds the whole argument up: nothing moves from potential to actual by itself. something already real has to make it happen. the room's air cools the coffee, the air conditioner cools the air, electricity runs the air conditioner, and electricity rests on atoms and the laws of physics.
\ picture a chandelier hanging from a chain. each link holds it only because the link above holds that one. no link has the power to hold anything on its own — every one of them borrows it. making the chain infinite explains nothing: without a ceiling, even an endless chain falls.
\ the same goes for the universe. even the quantum vacuum and the smallest particles change and are made of parts, so they need something to keep them in being moment by moment. they aren't the ceiling, just the finest links. and even if the universe had always existed, the question would remain: right now, what is holding the chain up?
\ logic leads to a source that isn't another link — pure act, with nothing still to become and no parts to hold together. having no potential, it can't change. having no parts, it isn't material. since all power to act flows from it, it is all-powerful. and since a cause can't give what it doesn't have in some way, it can't be less than what it produces: if intelligence and consciousness come from it, it isn't without them.
\ !!immutable, immaterial, intelligent, all-powerful: at the root of everything, we arrive at God!!.
