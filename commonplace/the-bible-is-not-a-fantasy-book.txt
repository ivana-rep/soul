the bible is not a fantasy book

\ calling the bible a fantasy book runs into two problems.
\ first, the bible isn't one book. it's a library, written over more than a thousand years by dozens of authors, holding completely different genres: law codes, lists of kings with the years they reigned, love poetry, historical and theological narratives, Ecclesiastes with its philosophical pessimism, real letters with a sender and a recipient. reading all of it as a single genre is like walking into a library, seeing the fiction shelf, and deciding the penal code must be fiction too.
\ second, and more important: Christianity stands on one event, the resurrection, told in the gospels. for most of the twentieth century scholars treated the gospels as something unique — popular writing of an anonymous community, with no parallel in the ancient world. then in 1992 Richard Burridge published a study at Cambridge, setting out to prove the gospels were not greco-roman biographies. he compared them with the ancient biographies that survive, and found he was wrong: they are. today that's the scholarly consensus.
\ and the ancient biography had a firm rule: it was written about a real person, usually recently dead, for readers who could still check whether the facts were true. when an ancient author did drift into legend, he said so — Plutarch, in his life of Theseus, warns the reader that from that point on the poets are in charge. the gospels carry no such warning. in its place there are names, places, dates, and witnesses still alive to be questioned.
\ !!fantasy does the opposite: it builds a world apart from ours, where by definition there's nothing to verify!!.
\ this doesn't prove the resurrection happened. but it shows that "the bible is a fantasy book" has no ground to stand on — it only reveals how little the texts have been read.
