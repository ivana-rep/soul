fasting is going without food, or something else you lean on, for a set time, to make room for God.
in scripture it's never a diet, a wellness trend, or a test of willpower: it's hunger turned into prayer.
Jesus takes it for granted: He says "when you fast", not "if you fast".

→ why it helps
    * when we're anxious we reach for something right away: food, the phone, the news, a distraction.
      fasting does the opposite: it stays with the discomfort and hands it to God.
    * the cravings aren't only for food: control, information, endless scrolling.
      fasting trains the one thing anxiety can't do: feel a want and not fill it at once.
    * the fast God asks for in Isaiah isn't a show of suffering: it loosens grips and opens hands.
    * it isn't self-punishment or proof of discipline. it's practice in trust, one small gap at a time.

→ how to start
    * small is enough: skip one meal and pray over one specific worry.
    * or set aside for a fixed time whatever feeds your restlessness: news, checking the phone, binge-watching.
    * when the discomfort comes, treat it as a cue to pray, not as a reason to find a substitute.
    * keep it between you and the Father: no one needs to know.
    * it won't bring instant relief, and that isn't the point. the point is turning to God in the gap.
